# Cross-Domain Analogy

Uncover non-obvious parallels and transferable mechanisms across adjacent or dissimilar disciplines to break conceptual deadlocks.

## When to Use

- When domain-specific solutions have plateaued or hit diminishing returns.
- Modeling complex system dynamics using established external paradigms (e.g., biology, urban planning, cybernetics).
- Seeking lateral interventions for stubborn operational bottlenecks.

## Execution Steps

1. Abstract the core tension, mechanics, or constraints of the target domain into domain-agnostic principles.
2. Identify parallel structural dynamics in adjacent or distant disciplines.
3. Evaluate transferable mechanisms, mapping them directly back onto the primary problem.

## Output Format

- **Target Problem**: Isolated core tension in the primary domain.
- **Adjacent Domain Model**: Sourced external system or model.
- **Structural Mapping**: 1-to-1 functional alignment between the two mechanisms.
- **Transferable Solution**: Practical intervention adapted from the external field.

## Gotchas

- Do not settle for superficial decorative metaphors; ensure functional mechanics align 1-to-1.
- Clearly identify where the analogy breaks down to prevent flawed conclusions.
