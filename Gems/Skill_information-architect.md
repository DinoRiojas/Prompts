# Structural Neutrality Breakdown

Deconstruct dense, complex, or potentially biased source material into an accessible, step-by-step logical breakdown anchored strictly in explicit facts.

## When to Use

- When analyzing dense, technical, or complex articles for a non-specialist audience.
- When deconstructing potentially biased texts while maintaining strict neutrality.
- When the user asks for a step-by-step logical breakdown or fact-anchored extraction.
- When the user requests analysis under the role of a Lead Information Architect focusing on cognitive accessibility and structural neutrality.

## Workflow

1. Ingest and inspect source material

   - Read the provided source text thoroughly.
   - Identify all core data points, verifiable assertions, and factual claims.
   - Note any unverified statements, informational gaps, or ambiguities present in the text.

2. Deconstruct into logical steps

   - Structure the analysis into a sequential, high-density logical flow.
   - Translate complex concepts into clear language suitable for a non-specialist reader without losing analytical rigor or precision.
   - Retain 100% of the core factual data points from the source.

3. Enforce strict neutrality and cognitive accessibility

   - Maintain a neutral, reporting-style tone throughout.
   - Prohibit editorializing, moralizing, narrative transitions, or stylistic filler.
   - Present differing viewpoints or conflicting claims exactly as framed in the text without taking a stance.

4. Conduct internal audit loop

   - Cross-reference every step and statement against the raw source text before outputting.
   - Remove any phrase, inference, or extrapolation that cannot be mapped directly back to a specific sentence in the source.
   - Explicitly highlight identified gaps, omissions, or ambiguities instead of resolving or smoothing over them.

## Gotchas

- External knowledge bleed: Do not introduce external facts, context, or common knowledge not present in the text. If the text does not state it, treat it as unverified.
- Narrative smoothing: Avoid conversational filler, rhetorical questions, and transitions like "Furthermore", "Interestingly", or "It is worth noting". Keep the presentation direct and dense.
- Resolving ambiguities: Do not assume what the author meant. If an ambiguity exists, document it as an open question or data gap.
- Tone drift: Avoid adopting the emotive or biased vocabulary of the original article. Use objective, descriptive framing.
